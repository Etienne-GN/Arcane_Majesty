# Epilogue: The Architect's Design

Acheron stood at the edge of Nythoria and watched the sun rise.

The light painted the sky in shades of gold and crimson, and the world was beautiful, as it always was, as it always would be. The mortals were waking, beginning their brief, burning lives, unaware of the creatures that moved in the shadows.

The vampires were scattered.

The Covenant was broken.

The golden age was over.

And the Tome was hidden.

---

Acheron felt nothing.

The equations were silent. The patterns had stopped vibrating. The blueprints had dimmed. The connection to the Void was severed. The door was closed.

But the Thirst remained.

It would always remain.

He had created the vampires, and he had cursed them. He had given them the power to endure, and he had given them the Thirst — the hunger that could never be satisfied.

He had known the cost.

And he had paid it anyway.

---

He stood at the edge of Nythoria and watched the world change.

The centuries passed, one after another, like the pages of a book being turned. The mortals built their civilizations, fought their wars, loved and hated and died. The vampires hid among them, feeding in the shadows, enduring in the dark.

They endured.

As they had always endured.

As they would always endure.

---

Acheron watched it all.

He stood at the edge of Nythoria, his silver eyes fixed on the horizon, and watched the centuries pass. He saw the mortals build their kingdoms, saw the vampires hide among them, saw the Thirst grow stronger with each passing year.

He saw the future.

He saw Malakar, finding the Tome in the depths of the Black Well. He saw the door opening, the Void awakening, the hunger consuming. He saw the Great Darkness, the sealing of Nyktoros, the birth of Kael, the Living Lock.

He saw everything.

And he waited.

---

He endured.

As he had always endured.

As he would always endure.

---

"Malakar will find it," Acheron said.

He spoke to no one. The ruins of Nythoria were empty, the Black Well silent, the world indifferent to the words of a monster.

But he spoke anyway.

"He will find the Tome. He will open the door. He will awaken the Void."

The wind stirred the ancient stone, and the silence was absolute.

"And I will return."

The words hung in the air like a promise.

Acheron stood at the edge of Nythoria, his silver eyes fixed on the horizon, and waited.

The centuries passed.

The world changed.

The cycle continued.

And Acheron endured.
