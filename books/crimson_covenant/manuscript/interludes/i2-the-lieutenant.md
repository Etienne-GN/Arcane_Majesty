# Interlude II: The Lieutenant

Vorenus stood in the ruins of the Sanguine Vaults and watched the energy die.

The conduits were sealed. The vaults were empty. The pulse that had hummed in the walls for centuries was silent. The Soul-Leak was dead, and with it, the sustenance that had kept the Thirst at bay.

The hunger returned.

Vorenus felt it in his blood, in his bones, in the Thirst that gnawed at the edges of his mind. It was the same hunger that had driven him to feed in the early days, before the Soul-Leak, before the vaults, before the golden age. The hunger that whispered in the blood and hummed in the bones. The hunger that could never be satisfied.

He was afraid.

Not of the hunger — he had learned to control it, to feed without killing, to take only what he needed. He was afraid of what the hunger meant. The Thirst was the Void's mechanism for harvesting souls. Every time he fed, the energy flowed through him, into the Phantom Realm, to Nyktoros. He was a conduit, a channel, a weapon aimed at the world.

He had always been a weapon.

Acheron had created him, given him the power to endure, and he had used that power to fight. He had fought in the War of the Ancients, in the schism, in the battle that had broken the Covenant. He had fought for Acheron, for the Covenant, for the chance to endure.

And now the Covenant was broken.

---

Vorenus had been loyal.

He had fought for Acheron when the fanatics rose, when Nyktoros's Herald led the charge into the deepest vaults, when the Phantom Realm bled through the cracks in reality. He had fought with the discipline of a soldier, the fury of a believer, the desperation of a creature fighting for survival.

He had watched Kael fall.

The warrior had been the first of the first four — the strongest, the bravest, the most loyal. He had led the charge into the deepest vaults, his silver eyes burning with the light of Auroria, his body moving with the speed and strength that the Void had given him.

And the fanatics had killed him.

Vorenus had felt it — a connection severed, a bond broken, a presence that had been with him since the beginning. Kael was dead. The warrior, the loyalist, the first of the first four. Gone.

The grief was unexpected.

Vorenus had not expected to feel grief. He was a soldier, trained to fight, to obey, to endure. He did not feel grief — he felt duty, loyalty, the quiet satisfaction of a battle won. But this was different. This was personal. Kael had been his companion, his friend, his brother in arms.

And now he was dead.

---

The aftermath was silence.

The Sanguine Vaults were empty. The conduits were sealed. The pulse that had hummed in the walls for centuries was silent. The Soul-Leak was dead, and with it, the sustenance that had kept the Thirst at bay.

The vampires scattered.

They left the vaults, left the estates, left the subterranean metropolis that Acheron had built. They broke into small cells, hid among the mortals, adopted the guise of humans. They were predators now, creatures of the night, living in the shadows of a world that did not know they existed.

Vorenus watched them go.

He stood in the ruins of the Sanguine Vaults, his silver eyes fixed on the sealed conduits, and felt the hunger grow in his chest. The Soul-Leak was dead. The Thirst was back. The sustenance that had kept the hunger at bay was gone.

He was alone.

Acheron was gone — departed for the Black Well, for the ruins of Nythoria, to hide the Tome beyond the reach of the Void. The other vampires were scattered, hiding among the mortals, surviving on the blood of the living.

Vorenus was alone.

---

He walked through the empty tunnels, his silver eyes scanning the darkness. The Sanguine Vaults had been beautiful once — a city unto themselves, lit by the energy of the Void, humming with the equations of the Tome. Now they were ruins, silent and empty, their chambers filled with dust and their tunnels filled with shadows.

Vorenus had helped build this place.

He had carved the tunnels, built the chambers, designed the conduits. He had worked alongside Acheron, his silver eyes gleaming, his body sustained by the energy of the Void. He had believed in the Covenant, in the Soul-Leak, in the chance to endure.

He had been wrong.

The Covenant was broken. The Soul-Leak was dead. The chance to endure had become a curse, a trap, a weapon aimed at the world.

Vorenus stood in the central chamber, where Acheron had studied the Tome, where the equations had hummed in the air, where the patterns had vibrated at frequencies that mortal ears could not parse. The chamber was empty now. The Tome was gone. The equations were silent.

But the hunger remained.

---

He thought about Acheron.

The creator, the First Vampire, the one who had stolen the forbidden texts and defied the High Council. He had created the vampires, built the Covenant, carved the Soul-Leak into the stone. He had given them the power to endure.

And then he had abandoned them.

Vorenus understood why. The Tome had to be hidden. The Soul-Leak had to be stopped. The connection to the Phantom Realm had to be sealed. Acheron had done what he had to do, what was necessary, what was right.

But the cost was high.

The vampires were scattered. The Covenant was broken. The golden age was over. The Thirst was back, and the hunger was growing, and the Void was stirring in the depths.

Vorenus stood in the ruins of the Sanguine Vaults, his silver eyes fixed on the darkness, and felt the hunger gnaw at the edges of his mind. He was a soldier, trained to fight, to obey, to endure. But endurance was not enough. Not anymore.

He needed to survive.

And survival meant feeding.

---

The first feeding was the hardest.

Vorenus left the Sanguine Vaults at night, when the mortal world was asleep and the shadows were long. He moved through the forests, his silver eyes scanning the darkness, his body moving with the speed and strength that the Void had given him.

He found a village on the edge of the Emerald Fields.

The mortals were asleep, their bodies warm, their life force pulsing in their veins. Vorenus stood at the edge of the village, his silver eyes fixed on the sleeping forms, and felt the hunger grow in his chest.

He was a soldier, trained to fight, to obey, to endure. But the hunger was not a thing that could be fought, obeyed, or endured. It was a force of nature — a hunger that gnawed at the edges of the mind, that whispered in the blood, that grew stronger with every passing moment.

He fed.

The life force flowed into him, warm and sweet, filling the void that the Soul-Leak had left. The hunger subsided, temporarily, and Vorenus felt the strength return to his limbs, the power return to his veins.

He was a predator.

He had always been a predator.

But now the prey was all he had.

---

Vorenus returned to the Sanguine Vaults at dawn.

He stood in the central chamber, his silver eyes fixed on the sealed conduits, and felt the hunger subside. The feeding had sustained him, temporarily, but the Thirst would return. It always returned. It was a part of what he was, a part of the Void, a part of the curse that Acheron had created.

He thought about the future.

The Covenant was broken. The vampires were scattered. The golden age was over. But the world continued, as it always continued, indifferent to the rise and fall of empires.

Vorenus would endure.

He would feed, as he had always fed. He would hide, as he was hiding now. He would survive, as he had always survived. The hunger would grow, the Thirst would strengthen, the Void would stir in the depths.

But Vorenus would endure.

He was a soldier, trained to fight, to obey, to endure. And endurance was all he had.

He stood in the ruins of the Sanguine Vaults, his silver eyes fixed on the darkness, and waited for the night.

The hunger would return.

It always did.
