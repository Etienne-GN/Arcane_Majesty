# Interlude III: The Scholar

The scholar heard the rumors in a tavern on the edge of Nythoria.

He was young — mortal young, perhaps twenty-five years of age, with silver eyes and a hunger for knowledge that burned in his veins like a second heartbeat. His name was not important. He was one of many, a researcher, a seeker, a mortal who had found fragments of the old texts and could not stop reading.

The rumors were about a book.

A book that contained the blueprints of Nyktoros, the energy schematics, the patterns that described how to siphon void energy into mortal flesh. A book that had been hidden in the depths of the Black Well, in the ruins of Nythoria, in the place where the Void was strongest.

The scholar did not believe the rumors.

But he could not stop thinking about them.

---

He had found the fragments in the archives of the northern cities — scraps of parchment, faded ink, the remnants of a civilization that had been old when the mortal world was young. The fragments spoke of the Void, of Nyktoros, of the energy that slumbered in the depths. They spoke of the blueprints, the energy schematics, the patterns that described how to siphon void energy into mortal flesh.

They spoke of the Tome.

The scholar had studied the fragments for years, parsing the equations, analyzing the patterns, trying to understand the energy that pulsed in the old texts. He had found nothing concrete, nothing definitive, nothing that proved the Tome was real.

But the rumors persisted.

And the hunger grew.

---

He set out for Nythoria on a morning when the sun was bright and the sky was clear.

The journey took weeks, through the forests of the Emerald Fields, across the plains of the Heartlands, over the mountains of the Scarlands. The scholar walked alone, his silver eyes fixed on the horizon, his mind filled with the equations that hummed in the old texts.

He did not know what he was looking for.

He only knew that he had to find it.

---

The ruins of Nythoria stretched before him — ancient stone, broken walls, the remnants of a civilization that had been old when the mortal world was young. The air was thick with the energy of the Void, the equations humming in the walls, the patterns vibrating at frequencies that mortal ears could not parse.

The scholar felt it.

A pull, a tug, a vibration that hummed in his blood and resonated with the equations of the old texts. The energy was everywhere — in the stone, in the air, in the silence that was absolute. It called to him, beckoned him, drew him toward the center of the ruins.

Toward the Black Well.

---

The Black Well was a pit, an abyss, a hole in the earth that led to the depths where the Void was strongest. The stone around it was dark, smooth, carved with channels that drew the energy from the Phantom Realm. The air was cold, and the silence was absolute, broken only by the distant sound of running water and the faint, almost imperceptible hum of energy that vibrated in the walls.

The scholar stood at the edge, his silver eyes fixed on the darkness, and felt the pull grow stronger.

Something was down there.

Something old, something powerful, something that called to him in frequencies that mortal ears could not parse.

He did not descend.

Not yet.

He was a scholar, trained to observe, to analyze, to understand. He did not rush into the unknown. He studied it, measured it, catalogued it.

But the hunger grew.

---

He camped at the edge of the Black Well for three days, studying the ruins, parsing the equations, analyzing the patterns. The energy was everywhere — in the stone, in the air, in the silence that was absolute. It hummed in his blood, vibrated in his bones, resonated with the equations of the old texts.

On the third night, he heard a voice.

It was not a voice, exactly. It was a frequency, a pattern, a vibration that resonated with the fabric of reality. It called to him from the depths of the Black Well, from the darkness where the Void was strongest.

"Find me," it said. "Find me, and I will give you power beyond your dreams."

The scholar stared into the darkness.

The hunger grew.

---

He did not descend.

Not yet.

He was a scholar, trained to observe, to analyze, to understand. He did not rush into the unknown. He studied it, measured it, catalogued it.

But the voice was persistent.

It called to him every night, from the depths of the Black Well, from the darkness where the Void was strongest. It promised power, knowledge, the chance to become something more than mortal.

The scholar resisted.

For a while.

---

On the fourth night, he descended.

He climbed down the ancient stone, his silver eyes fixed on the darkness, his hands gripping the rock. The energy grew stronger with each step, the pull more insistent, the voice more urgent.

"Find me," it said. "Find me, and I will give you power beyond your dreams."

The scholar descended into the darkness.

And the darkness received him.

---

He found the Tome at the bottom of the Black Well.

It lay on a flat stone, its pages glowing with a light that was not light. The equations hummed in the air, the patterns vibrated at frequencies that mortal ears could not parse. The scholar reached out and touched it.

The Tome was warm.

The equations flooded his mind, the patterns unfolded like flowers in the dark. He saw the blueprints, the energy schematics, the patterns that described how to siphon void energy into mortal flesh. He saw the transformation, the Thirst, the curse that Acheron had created.

He saw everything.

And the hunger grew.

---

The scholar emerged from the Black Well at dawn, the Tome clutched to his chest. The sun rose over the horizon, painting the sky in shades of gold and crimson. He watched it from the shadows, his silver eyes fixed on the light.

He had found the Tome.

He had found the power.

And the hunger was only beginning.

---

He did not know what he would do with it.

He was a scholar, trained to study, to analyze, to understand. He did not seek power — he sought knowledge, the truth, the equations that hummed in the old texts.

But the Tome was different.

The Tome was not just knowledge. It was power, raw and terrible, the blueprints of Nyktoros, the energy schematics of the Void. It was the key to transformation, to transcendence, to the chance to become something more than mortal.

And the hunger grew.

---

The scholar set out for home, the Tome clutched to his chest, the equations humming in his blood. He did not know what he would do with it. He did not know what he would become.

But he knew one thing.

The hunger was only beginning.

And the world would never be the same.

---

He would find others, eventually.

Seekers, researchers, mortals with silver eyes and a hunger for knowledge. He would teach them, share the Tome, pass on the equations that hummed in the old texts.

One of them would be named Malakar.

And the cycle would continue.

As it always had.

As it always would.

The cost of knowledge was blood.

And the blood was his to shed.
