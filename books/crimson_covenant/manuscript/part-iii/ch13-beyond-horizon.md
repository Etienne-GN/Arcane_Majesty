# Chapter 13: Beyond Horizon

The journey to Nythoria took three months.

Acheron walked alone, the Tome pulsing against his chest, the equations humming in his blood. The hunger was constant, but he had learned to live with it. He fed when he had to, taking only what he needed.

He killed rarely.

But he killed.

The cost of survival was blood.

And the blood was his to shed.

The journey was long, and the land was vast, and the world was beautiful, as it always was, as it always would be. Acheron moved through it like a ghost, his silver eyes fixed on the horizon, the Tome pulsing against his chest. The equations hummed in his blood, the patterns vibrated in his bones, the blueprints pulsed against his chest. He could feel the Void beyond the horizon, the dormant intelligence, the hunger that slumbered in the depths.

He was running toward it.

Or away from it.

He could no longer tell.

He traveled through the forests of the Emerald Fields, where the first vampires had been created. The ancient trees towered above him, their branches woven together in a canopy that blocked most of the sky. The air was thick with the smell of pine and earth and the faint, sweet scent of growing things. Or away from it.

He could no longer tell.

The forest floor was soft beneath his feet, carpeted with moss and fallen leaves, and the light that filtered through the canopy was green and dappled, shifting with the wind. He passed the clearing where the Blood Ritual had been performed — the stone altar, the ancient runes, the moss-covered granite that had once hummed with the energy of transformation. He did not stop. He did not look. He walked past, his silver eyes fixed on the darkness ahead, and felt the weight of what he had done settle on his shoulders like a cloak of lead.

The forest thinned as he moved south, giving way to the open plains of the Heartlands. The landscape was vast and flat, stretching to the horizon in every direction, broken only by the occasional copse of trees or the distant silhouette of a mortal village. The sky was wide overhead, a vault of cloud and pale light, and the wind swept across the plains with a sound like distant water.

Acheron walked through the heartland, his silver eyes fixed on the horizon, and felt the world open around him. The mortals were there — farming the land, building their villages, living their brief, burning lives. They did not see him. They did not know he existed. He was a shadow moving through their world, a creature of the night walking in the daylight, and the sun was warm on his skin — but he could not feel it.

The transformation had taken that from him.

As it had taken so many things.

He fed on the plains. A traveler on a lonely road, a merchant carrying goods between villages, a farmer walking home from the fields. Acheron moved through the darkness like a shadow, his silver eyes catching the faint light, his hands steady, his movements precise. The feeding was quick — a bite, a drain, a siphoning of life force that left the victim pale and weakened but alive. The energy flowed through him, into the Phantom Realm, to Nyktoros. The dormant entity stirred, fed, grew stronger.

The Thirst was satisfied.

For a time.

The traveler would wake at dawn, dazed and weakened, with a wound on his neck that he could not explain. He would stumble home, collapse into bed, and spend the next week recovering from an exhaustion that seemed to come from nowhere. He would tell his family that he had been ill, that he had caught a fever, that the road had been long and the sun had been hot.

He would not know the truth.

He would never know the truth.

Acheron walked on.
